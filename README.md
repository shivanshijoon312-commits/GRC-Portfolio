## Practice Case Study: Northside Allied Health

> **Note:** This is a **self-directed practice exercise** built on a **fictional organisation**. All names, systems and figures are invented. I created it to apply GRC methods end to end in a realistic Australian small-business setting.

**Scenario:** Northside Allied Health is a fictional physiotherapy and allied health clinic in Melbourne with 25 staff across two sites. It holds sensitive health information about roughly 8,000 patients in a cloud practice management system and runs on Microsoft 365. It has no formal risk process, no regular backup testing, and a privacy policy that hasn't been reviewed since 2021.

**Objective:** Find the clinic's biggest cyber and privacy risks, measure it against the Essential Eight (Maturity Level 1), and give the practice manager a prioritised, achievable plan.

| # | Work sample | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Risk Register](work-samples/01-risk-register.md) | Five risks scored by likelihood × consequence, with existing controls, treatments, owners and a heat map |
| 2 | [Essential Eight Gap Assessment](work-samples/02-essential-eight-gap-assessment.md) | All eight strategies assessed against ML1, with evidence expected, the gap observed and a recommendation |
| 3 | [Privacy Policy Review](work-samples/03-privacy-policy-review.md) | Policy checked against the Australian Privacy Principles and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme |
| 4 | [Audit-Style Finding](work-samples/04-audit-finding.md) | One High-rated finding written as Condition / Criteria / Cause / Risk / Recommendation |

**Frameworks applied:** NIST CSF 2.0 (functions and categories) · ASD Essential Eight ML1 · Privacy Act 1988 (APPs & NDB scheme) · NIST RMF concepts (categorise → monitor)
