# 03 · Privacy Policy Review: Northside Allied Health

> **Practice exercise. Fictional organisation.** The policy reviewed is an invented example. This is a learning exercise, not legal advice.

[← Back to portfolio](../README.md)

## Document reviewed

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Document | *Northside Allied Health Privacy Policy* (fictional), v1.2 |
| Last reviewed | March 2021 (review was due March 2023) |
| Reviewed against | Privacy Act 1988 (Cth): Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) 1, 5, 6, 11, 12 and 13; Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) scheme; OAIC guidance for health service providers |

## Strengths

- **Plain language.** Patients can read and understand it without legal training.
- **Clear purposes of collection.** It explains why health information is collected (treatment, billing, referrals), in line with APP 1 and APP 5.
- **Access and correction explained.** It tells patients how to request their records (APP 12 and 13).
- **Publicly available** on the clinic's website, as APP 1.4 expects.

## Gaps identified

| # | Gap | Why it matters | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **No named privacy contact.** The policy says "contact the clinic" with no role or contact details | Patients and the OAIC need a clear point of contact for questions and complaints | APP 1.4(e) |
| G2 | **Vague security wording.** It says only that information is "stored securely" | Gives patients no real assurance and staff no standard to follow; APP 11 requires *reasonable steps* | APP 11.1 |
| G3 | **No retention or destruction rules** | Records are kept forever by default, increasing the impact of any breach; Victorian health records rules set minimum retention periods | APP 11.2; Health Records Act 2001 (Vic) |
| G4 | **No data breach section** | The NDB scheme requires assessing a suspected breach within 30 days and notifying the OAIC and affected individuals if it is an *eligible data breach* | Privacy Act Part IIIC |
| G5 | **Overseas disclosure not addressed.** The cloud PMS may store data offshore | APP 1.4(f)–(g) requires stating whether information is likely to be disclosed overseas, and to which countries | APP 1.4, APP 8 |
| G6 | **Review overdue by more than 3 years** | An out-of-date policy may not reflect current systems (e.g. telehealth, the cloud PMS) | Good governance practice |

## Recommended improvements

1. **Name a Privacy Officer** (e.g. the Practice Manager) with a direct email and phone number. *(G1)*
2. **Describe security safeguards in plain terms**, such as MFA, encryption, role-based access and staff training, and link to an internal Information Security Policy. *(G2)*
3. **Add a retention and destruction schedule** that meets Victorian health records requirements, including secure disposal methods. *(G3)*
4. **Add a data breach response section** that explains how breaches are assessed and when patients and the OAIC will be notified, and link it to an internal Data Breach Response Plan. *(G4)*
5. **Confirm where the PMS vendor stores data** and state any overseas disclosure. *(G5)*
6. **Set an annual review date** and a trigger to review the policy after any new system or significant incident. *(G6)*

## Priority

| Priority | Items |
|---|---|
| High | G4 (breach response), G5 (overseas disclosure) |
| Medium | G1, G2, G3 |
| Low | G6 (fixed as part of this update) |
